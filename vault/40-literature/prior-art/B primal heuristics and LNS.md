# Prior art B — learned primal-side methods in MIP (primal heuristics, heuristic scheduling, diving, solution prediction, LNS)

Sweep date 2026-09-23. Scope: learned / neural / bandit control of the **primal side** of MIP solvers, 2014–2026, measured
against our question: *has a learned model already performed the solver-side function we give Laya — deciding heuristic
effort / solver settings per instance or during the solve?*

## 3. Closest prior art to our project

The primal-side literature splits into (a) learned **components** — predict-and-fix, neural diving, learned LNS
neighbourhoods, learned start heuristics (#12–#33) — that add a new heuristic to a solver, and (b) **control of
existing heuristic effort**, which is our function. In (b) the closest works are: Khalil et al. 2017 (#1), a learned
classifier deciding per node whether to run a heuristic — a learned mid-solve effort decision, but binary, per heuristic,
per node, and single-solver; Chmiela et al. 2021 (#2), a heuristic schedule learned offline per problem class with the
primal integral as target — the closest to "learn the right static heuristic setting", but a class-level schedule, not a
per-instance choice among whole-solver configurations; and the online-bandit line — Hendel et al. 2019 / ALNS (#4, #5),
Chmiela et al. 2023 (#3), Balans / ParBalans (#9, #10) — which is exactly the mechanism of our online bandit agent. Hendel's
solving-phase work (#6–#8) is the direct precedent for **switching settings mid-solve by phase**, hand-crafted rather than
learned. None of these uses a pretrained text encoder / foundation model as the decision maker, none chooses across
solvers (HiGHS / SCIP / cuOpt), and none isolates static choice from switching the way our C3 decomposition does. Two
points to cite explicitly: Balans reports that adaptive switching beats the best single neighbourhood, which is in
tension with our "switching adds ~2 %" (different granularity: neighbourhoods inside ALNS vs whole-solver emphasis
settings), and the ML4CO primal-task winner (#11) used hand-crafted classic heuristics on item placement — consistent
with our finding that the right static heuristic emphasis, not a learned heuristic, carries that family. The only
foundation-model primal-side work found, LLM-LNS (#21), uses an LLM offline to *write* neighbourhood-selection code,
not to decide per instance or during the solve.
